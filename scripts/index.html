ROLLED · DARK DUNGEON ROMPG

Warlocked

This is the next chapter of Rolled. Flay Queen Flay was the first.

Rolled is Dungeon RomPG. It’s romance in a dungeon setting.

It shifts between levels of the fantasy dungeon and the actual play scene. The scene shifting allows the series to have all the emotional depth and spice of a romance, combined with the mechanics and joys of a role-playing game. The dungeon RPG is familiar to most.

This series is definitely dark romance, so it’s most appropriately called Dark Dungeon RomPG. That’s a mouthful. In this episode, Ashlyn gets a mouthful a well.

In the actual play, she’s reminded of the terms of her pact. She is a vessel and a gateway. One day a week, she has to empty her mind and prepare herself ritualistically for occupation by her Patron.

The DM comments on this, blurring the line between the RPG and reality.

She’s reminded of the price for this – she has to retain her calm. It’s necessary to be a vessel. That isn’t easy for her; she has a strong sense of justice and doesn’t want to be ignored.

Characters for Casting

1. Ashlyn (Fem)

She is played as snarky, defiant, and whip smart in Act One.

She is in the midst of action, heated and ready for combat in Act Two. She is battling an orc chieftain to free his prisoners.

She is much changed in Act Three.

PEAKS AND VALLEYS: She undergoes a few evolutions in the course of the script. She goes from sassy and snarky, to violently defiant in an action movie way, to really quite cowed.

SUBTEXT: She is very much fascinated by her dire accounts of the Orcs.

RATING: R

2. DM (Masc)

He is accommodating in Act One. He receives and weathers the storms of Ashlyn’s rage.

He lights up more in Act Two.

His character evolution in Act Three is substantial.

PEAKS AND VALLEYS: He gradually transforms from a neutral game mechanic to an actual element in the game.

SUBTEXT: Although he is accommodating, there is steel beneath.

SUBTEXT: While he appears to bend to Ashlyn’s foibles, he knows how they can be leveraged.

RATING: R

3. Asta (Masc)

Asta appears as a voice in Ashyln’s head. He appears as an Archfey Patron – cold, but playful. The transformation is within the story.

RATING: R

4. Orc Boss

The Orc Boss is the antagonist for Act Two. He is beefy, powerful, and played more or less like a high fantasy gym bro.

RATING: PG-13 FOR COARSE LANGUAGE.

5. Captive

Ashlyn discovers the captive. She is frustrated by what seems to be deeply rooted Stockholm Syndrome. If you’re familiar with horror, you might think of Amanda Young in the Saw Franchise.

RATING: R
