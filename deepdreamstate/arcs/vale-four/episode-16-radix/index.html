Summary

Radix is the 43rd total episode of the desire horror audio drama, Deep Dream State. It is Season 4, Episode 16 and begins Grassroots, the final Cycle of Vale Four.

The Deep Dream State is all coming together, or it's all falling apart. It's hard to tell which.

Grassroots is the final Cycle of Vale Four. In a few episodes, the season will be resolved.

Each Cycle is about a different advertising phenomenon, made "thinkykinky." The show looks at a different fictionalized technology of control and asks the same question: "Why would anyone want this?"

We're finishing out the season with astroturfing. Astroturfing is the practice of making planned support look natural and organic. It's been around for decades, but it's really accelerated in an era of algorithmic mass manufacture of opinion.

Radix imagines some different takes on the show itself. Behind-the-scenes audio segments give listeners a whole different take on what the show means. Sections of the episode are designed to sound like they're off-mic bloopers or candid fragments.

They aren't. The possibilities outlined here are still within the fictional frame. The producer is not in a torrid dynamic with star VA Dizzy Dollie. Dizzy Dollie also ALWAYS gets her lines in on time. The script was not written by an enemies-to-lovers-to-enemies-to-lovers writing team. The Deep Dream State show might have a serious meta kink, but we know it's always important to stay rooted.

Radix brings Naia Anderson into the final movement of Vale Four, where advertising technology and manufactured agreement blur into conditioning. Tessa Fine and June Lowell move through shifting layers of performance while the familiar clicker makes the location of control difficult to pin down. Meg Aerin watches from another layer, turning repetition into a measurable behavioral pattern. The Grassroots Cycle turns astroturfing into desire horror, asking whether choices can still feel organic when the pattern has already been engineered.
